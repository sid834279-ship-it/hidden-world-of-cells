📚 Study notes

Quick revision notes for Class 11. Print or save this section as a PDF.

1. Cell theory

Living organisms consist of cells. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. New cells arise from pre-existing cells.

2. Prokaryotic cells

They lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Their DNA lies in the nucleoid region; bacteria are examples.

3. Eukaryotic cells

They have a membrane-bound nucleus and specialised organelles, as found in plants, animals, fungi and protists.

4. Plasma membrane

A selectively permeable boundary that helps regulate the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

5. Nucleus and ribosomes

The nucleus contains most genetic material. Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis and may be free or attached to rough ER.

6. ER and Golgi apparatus

Rough ER supports protein processing; smooth ER is involved in lipid synthesis. Golgi modifies, sorts and packages materials.

7. Mitochondria and chloroplasts

Mitochondria are important in aerobic respiration. Chloroplasts capture light energy for photosynthesis in plants and algae.

8. Plant vs animal cells

Plant cells typically have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues and a large central vacuole. Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts.

Study tip: learn each organelle by linking its structure to its function. These are quick revision notes, not a replacement for your textbook or teacher's syllabus.
